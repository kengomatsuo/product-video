## Notes

- Defines **timing** as the number of frames/drawings between two poses (how much time an action takes) and **spacing** as how the drawings are distributed within that time (the easing/acceleration curve). His standard demo: a coin moved across a page in exactly 1 second = 24 frames at 24fps.
- Teaches with **spacing charts** — a row of tick marks under the frame count showing where each in-between drawing falls, bunched near holds and spread out at full speed.
- Distinguishes **holds** (a pose held dead-still for several frames) from **moving holds** (a pose that keeps drifting almost imperceptibly so it doesn't look frozen/lifeless).
- Rule of thumb repeated across chapters: a plain hold under roughly 4 frames (at 24fps, ~1/6 s) reads as a hitch rather than a pose; a hold needs to run long enough for the audience to register the drawing as intentional, then moves into a moving hold if it needs to run much longer.
- Chapter titles directly on this topic: "It's All in the Timing and the Spacing," "More on Spacing," "Live-Action Reference," and the moving-hold discussion under "Holds."

## Quote (under 15 words)

"Timing is the time between the bounces of a dropped coin."
