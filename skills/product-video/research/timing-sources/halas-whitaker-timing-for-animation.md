## Notes

- The foundational classic-animation timing text (1981, still in print in a 40th-anniversary 3rd edition). Structured as short illustrated chapters, each answering one concrete timing question.
- Chapter "How Long to Hold?" — directly addresses how many frames a still pose needs before it reads as dead air vs. a deliberate beat; ties hold length to how much information/emotion the pose carries (a simple gesture needs less; a key story beat needs more).
- Chapter "Getting Into and Out of Holds" — covers the ease needed either side of a hold so the stop doesn't look like a mechanical snap.
- Chapter "Single Frames or Double Frames? Ones or Twos?" — the classic animation choice between drawing every frame ("ones," smoother but more labor) vs. every other frame ("twos," standard for most TV animation) and how that choice changes perceived speed and smoothness at the same nominal duration.
- General teaching device throughout: measure everything by frame count at a stated frame rate (traditionally 24fps) rather than abstract "fast/slow" language, so timing choices are reproducible.
