## Notes

- Shared doctrine across these practitioners (School of Motion's curriculum, Ben Marriott's "Master Motion Design" course, Jake Bartlett's After Effects courses) traces directly back to the Disney/Williams classic-animation principles above, translated into After Effects keyframe/graph-editor terms:
  - **Anticipation and overlapping action** get named as the two principles most worth deliberate practice for motion designers (Ben Marriott's course structure singles these out).
  - **Hold keyframes** (a distinct keyframe interpolation type in After Effects that snaps rather than eases) are taught as the practical tool for implementing a classic-animation "hold" — Jake Bartlett's beginner course has a dedicated lesson on them.
  - **Ease, don't linear-interpolate, almost everything** — the default advice in every one of these curricula is that a raw linear keyframe reads as mechanical; nearly every value change should ride a graph-editor ease curve, with only genuinely mechanical/robotic motion using true linear.
- No single numeric "hold for N frames" rule was found attributable to a specific named article from these three sources in this research pass — their guidance is taught through graph-editor demonstration rather than published tables, unlike Whitaker/Halas or the UI design systems above.
