# Cinemetrics — Average Shot Length (ASL) Data

URL: https://www.filmmakersacademy.com/glossary/average-shot-length-asl/
Database: https://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/

Fetched: 2026-09-24 (WebSearch)

## Notes

- **Average Shot Length (ASL)** = total runtime ÷ total shot count, in seconds. The Cinemetrics database (Yuri Tsivian et al.) crowdsources per-shot timestamps for thousands of films so ASL can be computed precisely rather than estimated.
- Historical trend: **Classical Hollywood** era averaged **8–11 seconds** per shot; **modern mainstream film** averages **4–6 seconds**.
- Range examples cited: Kubrick's *2001: A Space Odyssey* sits at an unusually long **~13 s ASL**; Greengrass's *The Bourne Supremacy* sits at an unusually short **~2.4 s ASL** — the two extremes of the deliberate-vs-kinetic editing spectrum.
- Directly useful for promo/trailer pacing as a reference floor: cutting *faster* than ~2 s consistently is already at the extreme "frenetic action" end of what feature film editing uses; most explainer/promo cutting that reads as "brisk but legible" sits closer to the 3–6 s modern-mainstream band, not the sub-2s extreme.
