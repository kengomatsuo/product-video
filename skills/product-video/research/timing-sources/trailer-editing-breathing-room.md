## Notes

- **Let the action finish before cutting to a static shot.** Cutting mid-motion (rather than at the start or end of a movement) reads as smoother because the eye is following the movement, not the edit; cutting to a shot where the same motion has already stopped is jarring.
- **"Decompressions"**: trailer editors deliberately hold a shot a beat longer than strictly needed at high-emotion moments, giving the audience a breath before the next information hit — the opposite instinct from "cut as soon as the point is made."
- In short-form (trailer-length) editing, there is much less room for these holds than in a feature — every second of breathing room competes directly with information the trailer needs to convey, so decompressions get reserved for the 1–2 beats that matter most (usually right before the title card or the button/tag at the end).
- Practical rule surfaced across multiple practitioner sources: don't cut on the peak of an action — cut just after it, so the payoff registers before the scene changes.

## Quote (under 15 words)

"Let the movement come to an end before you cut."
