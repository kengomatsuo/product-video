# Walter Murch — In the Blink of an Eye

URL: https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/walter-murch-rule-of-six/

Fetched: 2026-09-24 (WebSearch)

## Notes

- Murch's core observation: the rate at which people **blink** in ordinary conversation is statistically close to the rate at which a well-cut scene wants to change shots — a blink is the mind briefly disengaging to process what it just saw, and a cut can do the same job.
- He proposed this as a *diagnostic*, not a stopwatch rule: if a viewer would naturally blink around where you're placing a cut, the cut is probably landing at a natural break in attention; cutting mid-thought (where a blink wouldn't happen) reads as jarring regardless of shot length.
- His "Rule of Six" ranks what makes a cut work, weighted: **Emotion 51%, Story 23%, Rhythm 10%, Eye-trace 7%, Two-dimensional plane of the screen 5%, Three-dimensional space of action 4%** — i.e. timing/rhythm is real but subordinate to whether the cut serves the emotional and story beat.
