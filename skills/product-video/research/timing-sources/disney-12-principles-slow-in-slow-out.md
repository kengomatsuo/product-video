# Disney's 12 Principles of Animation — Slow In / Slow Out & Timing

URL: https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/slow-in-and-slow-out-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/
Also: https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-are-the-12-principles-of-animation/

Fetched: 2026-09-24 (web search, Animation Mentor + StudioBinder summaries of Frank Thomas & Ollie Johnston's "The Illusion of Life")

## Notes

- "Slow in, slow out" (a.k.a. ease-in/ease-out): more drawings are clustered near the start and end poses of a movement, fewer in the middle, so the object accelerates out of a hold and decelerates into the next one.
- This is a spacing rule, not a fixed duration rule — it says *where* the in-between frames go relative to a given total frame count, not how many frames to use.
- Companion principle "Timing" (one of the 12) is the frame-count decision itself: how many frames/drawings an action gets determines whether it reads as heavy/slow or light/fast — e.g. a fast take might be 2-3 frames, a considered gesture 12-24 frames at 24fps.
- Practical framing used by teaching sites: a run doesn't snap to full speed instantly — the character eases up to speed over several frames and eases down before a full stop, mirroring real inertia.

## Quote (under 15 words)

"Slow-ins and slow-outs soften the action, making it more life-like."
